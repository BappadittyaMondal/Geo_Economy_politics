Forex analysts attributed the rupee&#039;s strength to foreign institutional investors&#039; unabated chase for higher returns amid a weaker American currency and waning AI-related stock rallies in the U.S. and markets like Taiwan and South Korea. Also Read: Why is the Indian rupee falling? Lower crude oil prices and a weak dollar also supported the Indian currency, they said. Foreign investors pumped in ₹84,290 crore in Indian equities over nine trading sessions in November 2-13, marking a strong comeback after two consecutive months of net outflows, according to depository data. The latest inflows came after net withdrawals of ₹34,843 crore in October and ₹35,861 crore in September. With the latest inflows, cumulative FPI outflows from Indian equities have narrowed to around ₹2.1 lakh crore in 2026 from nearly ₹3 lakh crore a few weeks ago. At the interbank foreign exchange, the rupee opened at 93.25 and touched an intraday high of 92.65, climbing above the 93 level for the first time since April 10, when it closed at 92.83 against the U.S. dollar. On Thursday (November 19), the Indian currency settled 9 paise higher at 93.32 against the U.S. dollar. With Friday&#039;s (November 20) increase of 67 paise, the unit has logged a sharp appreciation of over 1.6% in the past five back-to-back sessions since the closing level of 94.19 against the greenback recorded on November 13. Anuj Choudhary, Research Analyst, Mirae Asset Sharekhan, said the rupee rose sharply, breaching the 93-a-dollar mark on a surge in the domestic market and fresh FII inflows. An overnight fall in commodity prices also supported the rupee. However, a strong dollar capped sharp gains. &quot;We expect the rupee to trade with a positive bias on strength in the domestic markets and sustained foreign inflows. Falling crude oil prices and other commodities also supported the rupee as they may ease import bills,&quot; Mr. Choudhary said, adding, &quot;Traders may take cues from revised consumer sentiment data from the U.S. The USD-INR spot price is expected to trade in a range of ₹92.40 to ₹93.&quot; The dollar index, which gauges the greenback&#039;s strength against a basket of six currencies, was at 103.87, lower by 0.08%. Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, was trading 1.82% lower at $90.31 per barrel in futures trade. In the domestic equity market, Sensex surged 897.96 points, or 1.08%, to settle at 83,919.71, while the Nifty climbed 286.30 points, or 1.12%, to 25,800.60. Both the indices ended the preceding session with more than 1% gains. Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) bought equities worth ₹7,411.41 crore on Thursday (November 19), according to exchange data.
