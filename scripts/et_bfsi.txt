Share on X Share on Telegram Share on Facebook By Nimesh Vora MUMBAI, - The Indian rupee on Friday is expected to largely shrug off mounting global risk aversion as a surge in foreign inflows into Indian assets offsets the traditional pressure that a deterioration in sentiment exerts on emerging-market currencies . The rupee is expected to open in the 93.28-93.34 range, per traders, having settled at 93.29 to the dollar on Thursday. Foreign investors continued to pour money into Indian equities in the face of mounting pressure on the global AI trade, buying more than $800 million worth of shares on Thursday. Their net purchases this month have now crossed $12 billion. Advt The inflows have helped lift the rupee by 1.75% this month, even as the dollar index has strengthened on bets of a Federal Reserve rate hike and safe-haven demand. The rupee is Asia's best-performing currency this month, underscoring how strong foreign portfolio inflows are helping it defy broader global headwinds. "The question now is how long this unusual dynamic, in which global risk aversion is driving flows into Indian assets and supporting the rupee, can persist," a currency trader at a bank said. "There will come a point when the deterioration in global sentiment becomes too severe for the currency to shrug off." CAPITAL ROTATION Global risk aversion is prompting investors to reallocate funds towards Indian assets, turning an external headwind into a source of support for the rupee. The previous market darlings of South Korea and Taiwan have come under heavy selling pressure as the global AI investment frenzy loses momentum, with their equity gauges falling as much as 20% this month, while Indian equities have rallied more than 10%. The unwinding of the AI trade continued on Thursday, with US equities tumbling and Asian markets following suit. Equity benchmarks in South Korea and Taiwan fell more than 4%, extending the selloff in markets that had been major beneficiaries of the AI-driven rally.
